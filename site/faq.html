The things people ask, answered without hedging.

If an answer here is uncomfortable, it is still the answer. Nothing below is aspirational: it describes what the app does today, including the parts that are unfinished.

Getting started

Can guests play without installing the app?

No. Everyone who plays installs it, guests included. The invite link is a deeper://join/CODE link, and a custom scheme only resolves on a device where the app is already present, so it saves typing the four character code rather than saving the install. There is no browser version and no web fallback: the join flow runs over a live socket, so a guest has to be running the real app to answer, react and see the reveal.

This is the single biggest thing missing from the product right now. A web client is the obvious fix, and until it exists the honest answer is that bringing a new person into a room costs them an install.

Do the same ten questions come up every time?

No. Each game draws ten at random from the hundred in that deck, and the draw avoids anything the last ten games of the same deck used, so it takes ten games to come all the way round. Within a game there are never any repeats.

Worth being precise about how the hundred are built: every deck is twenty base prompts, each written five ways. So a deck has a lot of range, and less variety than the headline number suggests.

How many people can play?

Two to eight. Three of the decks are capped at exactly two, because their questions assume there is one other person in the room: Couples, How Well Do You Know Me, and First Date.

Do we all have to be on the same wifi?

No. The reveal is synchronised by a server, not by the phones talking to each other, so people in different places can play the same room as long as each phone can reach that server.

What if somebody's connection drops mid game?

The room waits for them instead of revealing without them, and their seat keeps its sealed answer. Reopening the app puts them back in the room on the same question. This is the most common cause of a stalled game, and it is deliberate: revealing early would let the last person read the room before answering.

Can I leave without ruining it for everyone else?

Yes. A guest who leaves takes only themselves out of the room. If the host leaves, hosting passes to the next person still connected rather than ending the game.

Cost

What does it cost?

Hosting is free for the first ten games on every account, which is enough for most couples to find out whether they like it. DEEPER+ is a one-off unlock that removes the limit, opens the three premium decks, and removes the between-games ad break. Prices come from the store, so what you see at the till is the real price in your region.

Who gets charged for a game?

Only the host, and only once per room. Opening a room is free, so a group can gather, pick a deck, argue about it, and only then spend anything. Guests are never charged for anything, and a host who reconnects or reloads the app is not charged twice.

Are there ads?

One short break between games, for free hosts only. Never during a question, never during a reveal, never to guests, and never to DEEPER+. There is no ad SDK in the app at the moment, so in practice there is no ad break either. See the privacy policy for where that sits.

What is DEEPER+ exactly?

Three things: unlimited hosted games, the First Date, Midnight Conversations and After Dark decks, and no ad break. It does not unlock extra questions, because the catalogue is already eight hundred of them.

Privacy and data

What happens to the answers I write?

They are held on your phone, and in an encrypted room cache that expires two hours after the room is created whether or not you finish. They are deleted the moment the game ends. They are never written to the database, never logged, and never sent to analytics. There is no answers table in the schema, which is the part that actually guarantees it.

So can someone read my answers later?

No. The cache is AES-256-GCM encrypted under a key the server holds, and it is removed at the end of the game. If the encryption key is ever missing, the server refuses to store answers rather than quietly writing them in plain text, so the failure mode is a lost round rather than a leaked one.

Do you track me across apps?

No. The app generates a random string on your phone, keeps it in local storage, and uses it as an account handle. It reads no IDFA, no IDFV, no Android Advertising ID, no IMEI and no MAC address. It also ships no advertising or analytics SDK, and it does not declare an iOS tracking permission string.

What do you keep after the game is over?

Enough to stop a host charging the same room twice, and enough to count how many games have been played. Your display name, your room membership, which reactions you tapped and when. That is deleted on a ninety day cycle. Your account, your purchases and your premium status are not deleted on a timer, because deleting a purchase record would be a bookkeeping problem rather than a privacy improvement.

Can I get rid of my data?

There is no in-app delete button yet, and no account email to write to, which is a real gap. Uninstalling the app makes you unidentifiable, because the only link between you and the server is that random string on your phone. The policy sets out what happens to an erasure request when one arrives.

Is it safe for younger players?

After Dark is age gated and asks for confirmation before the room is created. The other seven decks are general audience, but none of them are written for children specifically, and DEEPER is not certified as child-directed. Read the policy for the detail on that.

The app itself

Which devices does it run on?

iOS and Android, built with Expo. The deep link that joins a room from an invite only resolves on a real installed build, not the Expo Go preview app, so testing an invite means installing a development build first. That is also true for the person receiving the invite, which is the limitation covered above.

Is it in the stores?

Not yet. There is no submitted build, which is why the buttons on this site are deliberately disabled rather than linking to a page that would 404. Nothing on this site is a demo: it describes the app's real behaviour, and there is nothing to install.

Does it work offline?

No, and it cannot. A simultaneous reveal needs a server holding the authoritative state of the room, so the game tells you plainly when it cannot reach it rather than letting the room quietly drift out of sync.

Is there a scoreboard?

Only in How Well Do You Know Me?, where a point is scored each time your prediction of the other person's answer matches what they actually said. The other seven decks have no score on purpose. The how to play page covers the difference.

Something is broken. How do I report it?

There is no support address published yet, and no crash reporting SDK wired up, so a report has to come through whoever shared the build with you. That is another honest gap rather than a policy: it is listed here so nobody waits for a reply that has nowhere to arrive.

Still want to try it?

Two phones, one room code, ten questions. The rest of this site explains exactly what happens.
