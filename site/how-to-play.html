How to play Decks Questions Privacy

From a room code to a scoreboard in about fifteen minutes.

One question lands on every phone at once. Everyone answers it alone. Then a shared countdown opens every sealed answer at the same second. That is the entire game, and everything below is detail.

Before you start

Two phones
The minimum. Everyone plays on their own device, so there is no shared screen and no passing a phone around.
One reachable server
Every phone has to reach the game server, because the server is what makes the reveal simultaneous. Phones do not talk to each other.
An invite link, if the app is there
The link is a deeper:// URL, so it opens DEEPER straight into the lobby on a phone that already has it. It saves typing the code. It does not save installing the app, because there is no browser version to fall back to.

Six things happen, in this order, ten times.

  1. 01

    Pick a deck and create a room

    Choose from eight decks. The deck fixes the tone for the whole game, so pick it before anybody arrives rather than arguing about it in the lobby. Set a display name and you are the host.

  2. 02

    Send the code or the link

    The lobby shows a four character room code, big enough to read across a table. There is a copy button and a share button that sends the invite link, which drops someone straight into your lobby.

    K3QF
  3. 03

    Wait for the room, then start

    Players appear in the lobby as they join, with the host marked. The room will not start with fewer than two people. When the room is full enough, the host taps Start conversation and the first question arrives everywhere at once.

  4. 04

    Answer in private

    Type up to a thousand characters. Your answer is sealed the moment you submit, and the screen tells you so. You cannot see anyone else's answer, and nobody can see yours. The app is waiting on the slowest phone in the room, on purpose.

    Why the wait matters

    If the room revealed as soon as the first person submitted, the second person would be answering while already able to guess what they should write. Sealing everything and opening everything together is what keeps the answers honest.

  5. 05

    Count down, then reveal together

    When everyone has answered, the room counts down from three and every phone opens its answers on the same beat. Tap ready when you want to go, and the countdown starts as soon as the room is ready.

  6. 06

    React, score, go again

    Four reactions are available: love it, thats funny, didnt expect that, and tell me more. Then the next question arrives. After ten questions you get a scoreboard, which only means something in the prediction deck.

    Love it 3 Thats funny 1 Didnt expect that 2 Tell me more 1

Every deck is standard. One of them is a game instead.

The deck you pick decides which of these you get. You cannot mix them inside a room, because prediction changes what the screen is asking for.

Standard

Seven of the eight decks. Everyone answers the question about themselves, and everybody reads everybody.

  • Up to eight players, so it works for a couple, a flat, or a table of friends.
  • No score. The conversation is the point, and a scoreboard would turn it into a quiz.
  • Reactions are the only feedback the game gives you.

Prediction

How Well Do You Know Me? You write what you think the other person will say, instead of answering yourself.

  • Two players only, and every other person in the room watches.
  • One point per exact match, case and spacing ignored, so a real answer is never lost to a typo.
  • The running score is visible to both players from the first question.

What the app actually enforces.

These are not suggestions. Each one is checked by the server, and the game refuses to start rather than bending them.

Enforced at the server, not just in the interface.
RuleWhat happens
Players per room Two to eight. Couples, How Well Do You Know Me, and First Date are capped at two, because their questions assume there is exactly one other person in the room.
Questions per game Ten, drawn from the deck you chose. The tenth reveal ends the game and shows the scoreboard.
Answer length Up to a thousand characters. An empty answer is rejected rather than submitted blank.
Who can start The host, and only the host. A room with one person in it will refuse to start.
Who is charged Only the host, and only once per room. Creating a room is free, and a reconnect or a reload never charges a second time. Guests are never charged for anything.
Free allowance Ten hosted games per account to start with. When they run out you can still join other people's rooms, or unlock DEEPER+.
Decks Five are free. First Date, Midnight Conversations, and After Dark need DEEPER+, and the server refuses the room rather than quietly serving a free deck.
After Dark Age gated. The app asks for confirmation before the room is created, and the confirmation is required again to host it.
Leaving mid game A guest can leave without ending the game for everyone else. If the host leaves, hosting passes to the next person still connected, so the room carries on.
Dropped connection The room waits for you rather than revealing without you. Reopen the app and you land back in your seat on the same question, with your sealed answer intact.
Ads One short break between games, shown to free hosts only. Never during a question, never during a reveal, never to guests, and never to DEEPER+.
Your answers Deleted when the game ends. Encrypted while the game is live, and never written to the database at any point. The full policy covers the rest.

Three things that will annoy you, stated up front.

The room waits for the slowest phone

If somebody drops off mid game, the reveal does not go ahead without them. That is deliberate, and it is also the most likely reason a game stalls. Reopening the app fixes it; leaving the room does not wait for anyone.

Everyone needs a working connection

The reveal is synchronised by a server, not by the players' phones talking to each other. If a phone cannot reach the server it says so plainly rather than quietly desyncing the room.

It is not in the stores yet

There is no App Store or Google Play link to follow, because there is no submitted build. Everything on this site describes the shipping app's behaviour, not a demo of it.
